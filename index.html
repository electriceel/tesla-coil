Safe keys

Safe work is the job where the paperwork comes first. A safe is where people keep the things worth stealing, so a caller who cannot document ownership is a caller you decline. Nothing here is about defeating a safe — the route is the serial number and the manufacturer, which costs the customer less and leaves the safe worth what it was.

Lishi guide

Which 2-in-1 reads which lock, and every vehicle in your database it opens. The counts come from the vehicle records themselves, so they grow as you add cars.
Before you pick one up. Read your numbers before you pull the tool — withdrawing it loses the reading. Count the wafers first so you know when you have them all. Light lubrication, never force: a lock full of grit misreads. A decode on a two-key-era car gives you a door key, not necessarily an ignition key — those are different codes. On most cars since roughly 2010 the ignition is a knob or is shielded, so the driver door is the lock you work. Clone tools read loose, and a depth misread by one is a wasted blank and a second trip.

VIN decoder

The offline decode (WMI, model year, check digit) always works. The NHTSA lookup needs signal and is the free government vPIC database — it will not give you a key code.
